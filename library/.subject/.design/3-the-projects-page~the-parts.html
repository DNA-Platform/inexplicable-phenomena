# The Parts of the Project

Specification

4. The parts

- 4.1 The parts, in the order of the work itself: you specify what you are looking for first; you try your best to identify the thing you are talking about justifiably; you build an intuition of what it might look like to find this blurry thing; and you express in narrative what it has meant to make progress.Open — awaiting a justification on the rendered order.

- 4.2 A justification is an annotation on the identified formalism that proves the specification is satisfied — relatively, not completely formally — and justifications frequently map to the citations on the project side.Open — satisfied when I3 carries its marks.

7. The structure of the parts

- 7.1 The specification is the agreement, so its unit is the clause — the atomic promise, uniquely numbered, citable; its form is the legal document. Satisfaction is judged clause by clause, which is why the clause is the unit.J1 · I1 — the clause form stands identified.

- 7.2 The identification states the thing, so its unit is the statement — a definition or a named object in the formal register, each carrying an anchor. It states and never narrates, because a justification must sit on a span that holds still.J2 · I2 — the statement form stands identified.

- 7.3 The justification is the justifiably, so its unit is the annotation with two mandatory references: the anchor it sits on in the identification, and the clause it claims satisfied — its writing arguing the claim in words. An annotation without a clause cite is an Idea, not a justification.J3 · I3 — the mark form stands identified.

- 7.4 The intuition pictures the blurry thing, so its unit is the essay paragraph in the deliverable's serif register, its references running outward to existing things and across to clauses and statements.J4 · I4 — the paragraph form stands identified.

- 7.5 The progress narrates, so its unit is the entry — byline and date as frontmatter, narrative prose as body, its references to the statements and clauses it narrates being its substance. Ruling out the past is an Archive mark that strikes an entry and never deletes it.J5 · I5 — the entry form stands identified.

Identification

I1 (The clause). A specification is a sequence of numbered, named sections holding dotted clauses; a clause is one promise, bearing one number, citable by it. It renders in the legal register: literal markdown, the source face. J1 · satisfies 7.1
One number, one promise, citable — the clause as 7.1 asks it.
I2 (The statement). An identification is a sequence of anchored statements; a statement defines a term or names an object, and holds still under annotation. It renders in the formal register: stated, never narrated. J2 · satisfies 7.2
Anchored and still — a span a mark can sit on, as 7.2 asks.
I3 (The justification mark). A justification is an annotation carrying two references — the anchor it sits on, the clause it claims — and a writing that argues the claim in words. Missing its clause, it is an Idea; the marks on this page are the form shown on itself. J3 · satisfies 7.3 · 4.2
Two references and an argument — relative proof, words plus citation; one mark may claim many clauses, and is a thing to view in its own right.
I4 (The essay paragraph). An intuition is serif prose in paragraphs, referencing outward to existing things and across to clauses and statements. The loosest form, held only to its references resolving. J4 · satisfies 7.4
Paragraphs that cite — blurry words on firm anchors.
I5 (The entry). A progress is a time-ordered sequence of entries; an entry carries author and date as frontmatter, narrative as body, references as substance. A struck entry remains. J5 · satisfies 7.5
Byline, date, narrative, references — and the past kept struck.

Intuition

P1 (The contract's ancestry). The specification descends from the service contract — agreed between buyer and seller, identifying the deliverable uniquely. A contract is honored or breached clause by clause, so the clause is the atom (7.1).
P2 (The talk page). The project stands to its deliverable as a talk page stands to its article — the same subject, the working face; the parts give it the spine a talk page never had (4.1).
P3 (The permutation). The parts keep recognizable forms inside one face — Sprint 129's test carried forward, identity preserved under the flip from writing to code (I1–I5).
P4 (The borrowed form), connecting P1 · P2. The back borrows an older form whose conventions already answer its questions — the contract for the specification, the talk page for the project. A relationship between intuitions is itself an intuition; the graph can tangle.
P5 (What finding looks like). Blurry still: every clause of section 7 carrying at least one justification, the coverage reading satisfied all the way down, and this page — the sprint's own project — the first place that reading happens (7.3).
