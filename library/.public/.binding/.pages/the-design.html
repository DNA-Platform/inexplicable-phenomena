The Top Page
Sketch 001, three forms of the top page
The first sketch asks what the top of the library should look like, from the bound print of the top book as the borrowed manual draws it. The page is appended to this chapter with its forms on a switch, and a form is a question, not a design. The forms: a, the page as bound, the cover in its box, the byline above it, the synopsis beside, the catalogue as a two-column table; b, a front, the masthead and the byline on one line, the name large, the synopsis as a standfirst, the catalogue as three shelves side by side and the chapter below; c, the subjects first, the catalogue standing above the synopsis in two columns under a small cover.
What the sketch found on its first runs: the header row of the catalogue, Book and What it is, is a row like the others and became a shelf of its own in form b until the sheet hid it by a rule more specific than the one that made the shelves, since a later rule of equal weight had won. And form c misfired: the masthead, the byline and the chapters are not siblings on the page, each standing in a container of its own, so an order given to them put the masthead last and left the catalogue under the synopsis; a form that moves the parts needs another mechanism than order. Nothing here moved into the manual.

The Manual's Page
Sketch 001, five forms of the manual's page
The first sketch of the manual's page asks how a chapter of Our Reference shows the file beside it, the code that is the part the chapter specifies, now that the manual is written as a specification. The listing itself is to look as it does in my own library's manual, measured off it on 17 November 2026: JetBrains Mono at twelve pixels on a line of twenty, the ink pale on a night of deep teal, the keywords sky, the strings amber, the names mint, the comments dimmed, the numbers in a column of brass, and that manual's light palette behind a switch. The container is this page's own, the editor's bar speaking in the page's label voice. The page is snapshotted off 4343's print of The Faces, its sheets inlined and its scripts stripped, and appended to this chapter with its forms on a switch; a form is a question, not a design. The forms: a, the plate, the editor standing in the chapter where the code figure stands today, at the measure, capped at seven tenths of the window and scrolling within, with light, wrap and lines on its bar and a full screen that takes the window; b, the back, the chapter's words alone on its front and the file on its back, flipped by the square at the title's right that The Content flips with, the title turning into the file's own name in the editor's face as the content's title turns into its literal; c, the spread, my library's container on this paper, the words at the window's left and the editor beside them to its edge, the height of the window; and on the table's page, t, the appendix, a section at the foot of the contents in the smaller voice, one row a file under the chapter it stands beside, and u, each file as a press under its chapter's row with the file's mark. In t and u a press on a file opens its chapter turned to the file.
What the sketch found on its first runs: the plate stood below the fold in every photograph until the switch learned to scroll to it, as a reader arriving at the code would; the spread gridded the pagination's container instead of the theme's and left the words centred, until the sheet was named by its place under the root; the figure on the print carries its highlighting already, 2,315 spans, and no line numbers, since the manual never asked for them, so the sketch numbers the lines as the code figure would; a chapter with two files beside it, The Paper's code and its preview tool, shows one tab, since only the code is a figure on the page; and on the phone the bar's four words wrap to a second line. Nothing here moved into the manual.
