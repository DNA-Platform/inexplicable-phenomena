The Notebook
The notebook takes the notes. Nothing in it is official: it is where rulings, analogies and specifications live while the textbook is not ready to state them. The notebook of the first subject is The Notebook of Arithmetic.
A chapter names its part with the Part annotation and opens with one provenance paragraph: where the note came from and on whose word it stands.
Only I am quoted. Everyone else — a paper, a session, a teammate — is paraphrased, with the source named.
Titles are short names, and no heading on the page repeats a title or another heading.
Every chapter gets its row on the table of contents and its clause in the synopsis, written in the same act.
A resource stands beside its chapter, named for it with a tilde.
One idiom is enforced by code: every quoted span of four or more words must stand in a conversation's message or a sprint record, and the checker beside The Sources proves it, failing the loop until every span is found. The rest are held by hand.
The Textbook
The textbook is the first place where official results go. A definition or a theorem enters it only as I have ruled it in the room; what I have not ruled stays a notebook note. The textbook of the first subject is The Textbook of Arithmetic.
Official results only: every chapter stands on a ruling recorded verbatim in the sprint records.
The official form is the algebraic one, ruled 12 November 2026; the prose stays in the notebook.
Results are stated and proved, and their references resolve into the subject's library of publications.
What a textbook entry is, as I have specified it so far, 12 and 15 November 2026: a chapter is an attempt to specify one mathematical object, and bears the object's title. It carries the specification of the object — the sovereign part, since we formalize specifications — an informal explanation, the formalism itself with a justification of how it satisfies the specification, and presentations in different contexts, each context itself specified. Citations run into the subject's library and into the subject's own notes. One aspect is viewable at a time. What is specified and not yet built stands as a placeholder for itself — the interpolation approach. The justification leans toward visible annotations, writing added to a part of a chapter and toggled in the UI; and the specification needs a form, since when it changes the formal structure and the justifications must change with it.
The components foreseen and not yet ruled: a form for the specification whose clauses have identity; the justification as an annotation sort whose note answers with writing, toggled by the theme; a placeholder for the interpolation; a context specification for each presentation; and the theme's rule that shows one aspect alone, on the front's precedent. The classes will stand with this chapter as its code once they are ruled; until then every idiom here is held by hand. The potential four chapters of 12 November stand retired in the subject's notes, and the textbook's first object is named: X, the symmetry of arithmetic, specified by my statement of work of 15 November 2026, its chapter the textbook's first — the identification standing as its own placeholder.
The Project
The project is the record of the work: what each sprint accomplished and what it leaves. The project of the first subject is The Project of Arithmetic.
One entry per sprint, written at the close, in the form of the entries before it.
An entry names what was built, refers into the notebook and the library where the work landed, and says what the sprint leaves.
Each entry has its row on the project's table; the sprint's full record lives in the branch library, not here.
Held by hand; the loop's prover walks every reference an entry makes.
The Library
The library of a subject holds the publications its work leans on. The first is The Library of Mathematics.
A publication is a chapter, keyed by author and year in the form of Gödel 1931, and the table lists the rows alphabetically by key.
A free text is held beside its chapter with its file and a plate of the first page; a copyrighted one is cited by address.
The chapter carries the citation and the notes; the citing books refer in by the key, and the chapter's writeup refers back to them.
Enforced by code: the prover in the branch library, the tool of the book Inexplicable Phenomena's third chapter, opens every row in a real browser, follows the plate to the held file or the address, and fails on a landing that does not answer. The keys and the rows are held by hand.

The Specification
The specification of a specification, which I ruled on 12 November 2026, written in its own form. For right now a synopsis is a title for the specification with a numbered list of lists, and in a book's synopsis we write the specification of what the book is supposed to be; the first is The Textbook of Arithmetic's.
1. The form
1.1 A specification is a title with a numbered list of lists.
1.2 Every item carries its number, and things are referenced across the specification by those numbers.
1.3 For right now, the specification of a book is written in its synopsis.
2. The two forms
2.1 A thing shows in specification form or in implementation form.
2.2 The specification and the thing it specifies move back and forth, each reachable from the other.
2.3 In a chapter the formalization, the explanation and the presentation are each specified, and each moves back and forth with its specification.
3. The justification
3.1 The implementation is annotated with justifications.
3.2 A justification says how the implementation satisfies the specification, citing items by number.
3.3 A justification is a visible annotation, toggled in the UI.
4. The interpolation
4.1 What is specified and not yet built stands as a placeholder for itself.
4.2 An item with no implementation to move to is the placeholder.
5. The permanence
5.1 Specifications are not thrown away; they stay, to be read and improved.
5.2 A change is made by changing the specification, so changes are made in the context of all the other parts.
The components of the library that show a thing in specification against implementation form, carry the movement between the two, and bear the justifications begin from this chapter, and they will stand beside it as its code. Until they do, the form is held by hand — the numbers written in the lines, the movement by mention.
The Content
My idea, 15 November 2026: a sort of thing that holds content, with a front and a back — on the back the specification, on the front the identification — like the two sides of a wikipedia page, toggled back and forth. It is not a type of chapter. The name, Content, is my maybe and stands until I settle it: the base library already exports a word Content, a Word's content, so the name is to be decided when the thing moves up — and it likely moves all the way up to the base library, played with here first. My frame of 16 November: the design is of the abstraction that applies to anything written in any book — a way of specifying anything a book holds — tested on the math textbook, with components for math textbooks specializing it where more structure is needed, and appendix chapters open to the textbook itself. The specification it shows on its back takes the form of The Specification.
The object model, said back to me from my words of 15 November 2026, amendable in place as I correct it.
1. The content
1.1 A piece of content is one thing with a front and a back: the identification on the front, the specification on the back.
1.2 It is not a type of chapter; it is a thing that holds content, and a chapter or a book carries one.
1.3 It shows one side at a time and toggles back and forth between them.
1.4 It opens in the object mode, the front; which side is canonical is overridable; and a book says in one word that it defaults to showing one.
1.5 It is a way of specifying anything written in any book; the math textbook is its test, and math-textbook components specialize it where more structure is needed.
1.6 The back shows the specification alone, the progress alone, or the two one on top of the other.
2. The parts
2.1 The identification, the front: the thing itself, as far as it is identified; where it is not yet identified, its placeholder stands — the interpolation.
2.2 The specification, the back: what the thing must be, in the specification's form — a title with a numbered list of lists, items cited by number.
2.3 The purpose: why the thing is specified, standing with the specification.
2.4 The project: each piece of content has one — the work that moves from the specification toward the identification.
2.5 The progress: the project has progress, the record of its determinations, open and closed; its form a narrative of independent things, which may refer to each other.
3. The authors
3.1 Every part has an author, so the team is embedded in the content.
3.2 The author of the book is the default author of every part.
4. The open determinations
4.1 The name: Content collides with the base library's word for a Word's content.
4.2 The toggle's mechanics: a turn of the book to the other side, or a flip in place; played for now as a flip in place — labels over hidden inputs, the chapter ruled by what is checked, no script — the ruling still mine.
4.3 Whether the purpose, the project and the progress are sides of their own or parts standing with a side.
4.4 Where it lives: played with at the library layer here, likely moved all the way up to the base library.
4.5 What a piece of content is and what its project is: the first play made them states of a four-mode bar, and they are not states — a project and a content are things, seen in the two sides of the chapter the content is in; the model stands to be respecified as I answer the team's questions, 16 November.
The first case already stands: The Symmetry of Arithmetic, whose back is a statement of work — a purpose, a specification in seven parts, a project in the search for X, a progress in the open determinations O1 to O11, and an author — and whose front, the identification of X, is a teaching sketch whose unfound parts stand interpolated. The model is read off that statement.
The components stand beside this chapter as its code, the play of 16 November. The general ones: Content, the bar of modes — a format said of its chapter, its note drawing the bar in place and its style wrapping the chapter, so the checked mode rules the sides — its canonical side a field a use overrides; Identification, Specification and Progress, the sides, each a section of the chapter bearing its name; and Interpolated, the word that stands for a thing until the thing is found. The math textbook's specializations: Theorem, counted within the chapter, and Proof, labelled. The first chapter of the Textbook of Arithmetic writes with all of them.
