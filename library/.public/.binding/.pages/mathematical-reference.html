The Notebook
The notebook takes the notes. Nothing in it is official: it is where rulings, analogies and specifications live while the textbook is not ready to state them. The notebook of the first subject is The Notebook of Arithmetic.
A chapter names its part with the Part annotation and opens with one provenance paragraph: where the note came from and on whose word it stands.
Only Doug is quoted. Everyone else — a paper, a session, a teammate — is paraphrased, with the source named.
Titles are short names, and no heading on the page repeats a title or another heading.
Every chapter gets its row on the table of contents and its clause in the synopsis, written in the same act.
A resource stands beside its chapter, named for it with a tilde.
One idiom is enforced by code: every quoted span of four or more words must stand in a conversation's message or a sprint record, and the checker beside The Sources proves it, failing the loop until every span is found. The rest are held by hand.
The Textbook
The textbook is the first place where official results go. A definition or a theorem enters it only as Doug has ruled it in the room; what he has not ruled stays a notebook note. The textbook of the first subject is The Textbook of Arithmetic.
Official results only: every chapter stands on a ruling recorded verbatim in the sprint records.
The official form is the algebraic one, ruled 12 November 2026; the prose stays in the notebook.
Results are stated and proved, and their references resolve into the subject's library of publications.
What a textbook entry is, as Doug has specified it so far, 12 November 2026: a chapter is about one formalism. It carries the specification of the formalism — the sovereign part, since we formalize specifications — an informal explanation, the formalism itself with a justification of how it satisfies the specification, and presentations in different contexts, each context itself specified. Citations run into the subject's library and into the subject's own notes. One aspect is viewable at a time. What is specified and not yet built stands as a placeholder for itself — the interpolation approach. The justification leans toward visible annotations, writing added to a part of a chapter and toggled in the UI; and the specification needs a form, since when it changes the formal structure and the justifications must change with it.
The components foreseen and not yet ruled: a form for the specification whose clauses have identity; the justification as an annotation sort whose note answers with writing, toggled by the theme; a placeholder for the interpolation; a context specification for each presentation; and the theme's rule that shows one aspect alone, on the front's precedent. The classes will stand with this chapter as its code once they are ruled; until then every idiom here is held by hand, and the four chapters of the Textbook of Arithmetic stand specified in this shape, each its specification and a placeholder.
The Project
The project is the record of the work: what each sprint accomplished and what it leaves. The project of the first subject is The Project of Arithmetic.
One entry per sprint, written at the close, in the form of the entries before it.
An entry names what was built, refers into the notebook and the library where the work landed, and says what the sprint leaves.
Each entry has its row on the project's table; the sprint's full record lives in the branch library, not here.
Held by hand; the loop's prover walks every reference an entry makes.
The Library
The library of a subject holds the publications its work leans on. The first is The Library of Mathematics.
A publication is a chapter, keyed by author and year in the form of Gödel 1931, and the table lists the rows alphabetically by key.
A free text is held beside its chapter with its file and a plate of the first page; a copyrighted one is cited by address.
The chapter carries the citation and the notes; the citing books refer in by the key, and the chapter's writeup refers back to them.
Enforced by code: the prover in the branch library, the tool of the book Inexplicable Phenomena's third chapter, opens every row in a real browser, follows the plate to the held file or the address, and fails on a landing that does not answer. The keys and the rows are held by hand.
The Design
The design book holds the subject's visual decisions as sketches. The first is Subjective Design.
A sketch is an HTML page beside its chapter, its forms lettered, each photographed at a desk's width and a phone's.
A sketch is drawn in the pages' own palette and never with the print's class names; the decided form is cited by its number when it is built.
A built design refers to the chapters that exemplify it.
Held by hand; the photographs stand beside the chapter as its record.
The Specification
The specification of a specification, ruled by Doug on 12 November 2026 and written in its own form. For right now a synopsis is a title for the specification with a numbered list of lists, and in a book's synopsis we write the specification of what the book is supposed to be; the first is The Textbook of Arithmetic's.
1. The form
1.1 A specification is a title with a numbered list of lists.
1.2 Every item carries its number, and things are referenced across the specification by those numbers.
1.3 For right now, the specification of a book is written in its synopsis.
2. The two forms
2.1 A thing shows in specification form or in implementation form.
2.2 The specification and the thing it specifies move back and forth, each reachable from the other.
2.3 In a chapter the formalization, the explanation and the presentation are each specified, and each moves back and forth with its specification.
3. The justification
3.1 The implementation is annotated with justifications.
3.2 A justification says how the implementation satisfies the specification, citing items by number.
3.3 A justification is a visible annotation, toggled in the UI.
4. The interpolation
4.1 What is specified and not yet built stands as a placeholder for itself.
4.2 An item with no implementation to move to is the placeholder.
The components of the library that show a thing in specification against implementation form, carry the movement between the two, and bear the justifications begin from this chapter, and they will stand beside it as its code. Until they do, the form is held by hand — the numbers written in the lines, the movement by mention.
