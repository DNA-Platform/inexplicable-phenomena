The Notes
The first sprint of arithmetic, opened and closed on 9 November 2026, when Doug named the subject: a paper he is working on, once called Goldbach versus Gödel, now arithmetic, with this project beside it to keep its sprints and plans, a textbook for its results and, in Mathematics, a library for the papers referenced.
What was accomplished
The structure, every book a stub that can be walked: the subject, this project, the textbook and the library, built and proven the day they were named. The team's book gained its chapterDoug, so that every book of this library is signed by him as the team's representative. Then the notebook, and the notes moved into it from Doug's own library, where the team had read and annotated six conversations with Claude, the planning of the paper, the semantics beside it, the symmetry of the coprimes, the long conversation from the finite proof to the paper's flow, the catalogue of a session with Gemini, and the logic axioms. The notebook is the specification of the publication, written from the most recent statement of the work backwards so that newer thoughts deprecate older ones: fifteen chapters in four parts, the publication's own parts from the preface to Goldbach, the standing rulings and documents, the sketches set aside beside the paper with his tag on each, and the formalism, the options the notes hold and the algebraic model of the subject of logic. Each note is his words in the form that stands, the spelling cleaned and nothing else changed, citing what it deprecated and the conversation and chapter it came from, and every quoted span is checked mechanically against the messages before the notebook is bound.
The formalism's part ended the sprint. His question on the running notebook, whether the algebraic model of the subject of logic had been captured, found it in his library in pieces and nowhere whole; it was written as the three identities, inferential, rational and logical, the excluded middle kept, the rules of inference under which consistency motivates the axioms on the page, soundness as the subject being formalizable, and his sharpening that the semantics are expressed through the identities and nothing else, an equivalent expression of logic except that consistency is not formalizable in it. The team's candidate for a compact expression of the model stands in that chapter, marked as the team's, for his correction.
What it leaves for the next
The compact expression, to be accepted or corrected by him, and then its formalization in PA. The organizing of the notes, which waits until he is happy with them. The papers, which go to the library of Mathematics as they are referenced: the conversations already name Gödel's paper of 1931, Feferman, Löb, Rosser, Rogers, Detlefsen, Kripke, Hofstadter's TNT, Girard, Lambert, Beltrami, Hilbert, Dedekind and Jurkat and Richert, and the next sprint is to find them as potential citations. The results, which go to the textbook once the notes they come from are organized. And the form of this project's entries, which this one sets by being first.
The Papers
The second sprint of arithmetic, opened and closed on 10 November 2026, on Doug's word that the next step is a way to add papers to the library and to see whether the papers and resources themselves can stand in it, the first resource Gödel's paper on formally undecidable propositions; then that references good for the project are collected, the book for the library designed, and the library released as the site it was always meant to be.
What the second sprint accomplished
A paper is a citable publication held in the library as a resource: the file itself beside a chapter of The Library of Mathematics that carries its citation and the notes Doug writes on it, the paper signed by its own author. The binder serves a document beside a chapter at its address as it serves a picture, never inlined, and the package gained a Document figure for the viewer, though the page built to his number does without it: the paper's page is minimal, a small picture of the paper's first page at the top left that opens the paper in another tab, the citation, the notes, and an opening for each translation. Gödel 1931 stands there in English, Meltzer's whole paper and Hirzel's two sections, and the notebook cites it in three places. The library's book was designed in four sketches of the design book, the paper's page and the cover decided by his numbers and built in the manual: the cover drawn as a book, its words its links, the title opening the contents, the author and the subject their books. The site's publishing was turned on, every push to publish the library, though GitHub had run none by the sprint's close. And the references below were collected, each with its address and its terms.
What the second sprint leaves
The citations for the paper itself, collected from these references and beyond them into the library as the next sprint, each a chapter of Doug's when he picks it; the German original beside Gödel's chapter from 1 January 2027, when it is public domain in the United States; the table's form as the library grows, alphabetical and tagged by subject in time; his notes on each paper; and the deploy, which runs once the repository's Actions settings allow it.

References the team finds good for the project
Chosen for a formalism of the notebook's The Options or a sentence of its The Subject of Logic that each bears on: the derivability conditions behind Löb, the provability logic that studies them, the algebraic descriptions of first-order logic that the semantics of logic in algebraic form would be measured against, and the metamathematics the paper's core is written in. None is downloaded this sprint; each is a chapter of Doug's when he picks it.
Hilbert and Bernays 1939, Grundlagen der Mathematik II, Springer, second edition 1970; the derivability conditions a provability predicate must satisfy, which the lifted predicate in PA and the identity's rule are measured against. Under copyright.
Smoryński 1985, Self-Reference and Modal Logic, Springer; the provability logic GL in full, where the empty proof's rule would be read. Under copyright.
Boolos 1993, The Logic of Provability, Cambridge; the same logic from its semantics, and the fixed points. Under copyright.
Halmos 1962, Algebraic Logic, Chelsea; polyadic algebras, an algebraic description of first-order logic, which the first order semantics the notes reach for would be compared with. Under copyright; no address.
Henkin, Monk and Tarski 1971, Cylindric Algebras, Part I, North-Holland; the other algebraic description of first-order logic, with an identity in its signature. Under copyright; no address.
Rasiowa and Sikorski 1963, The Mathematics of Metamathematics, PWN; Boolean algebras carrying classical logic and its completeness, the model the algebra with an identity adjoined departs from. Under copyright; no address.
Kleene 1952, Introduction to Metamathematics, North-Holland; the arithmetization and the first theorem's proof in the form most of the century read, with the ω-consistency the notes weigh. Under copyright; no address.
Gödel 1934, On undecidable propositions of formal mathematical systems, lectures at the Institute for Advanced Study taken down by Kleene and Rosser, in Davis 1965, The Undecidable, Raven, Dover 2004; the general recursive functions and Gödel's own restatement. Under copyright; no address.
Franzén 2005, Gödel's Theorem: An Incomplete Guide to Its Use and Abuse, A K Peters; what the theorems do not say, which a paper that reinterprets them must not say either. Under copyright; no address.
Halbach 2014, Axiomatic Theories of Truth, Cambridge, second edition; the typed and untyped truth predicates, where a truth value that is the absence of logic would be placed. Under copyright; no address.
The Publications
The third sprint of arithmetic, opened on 10 November 2026 at the second's close, on Doug's word that the next sprint looks at our notes and identifies publications to put in the library: the essentials, since he reviews each, one for each thing the notes lean on, and Turing besides, he and Feferman being the biggest names his reading of Gödel changes. Closed the same day.
What the third sprint accomplished
Fifteen publications, each a chapter of the library as Gödel 1931 is, keyed by author and year and listed alphabetically: Beltrami 1868, the model of the other geometry; Chen 1973, the theorem beside Goldbach; Dedekind 1872, continuity adopted as an axiom; Feferman 1960, the arithmetization and its intensionality; Gauss 1799, the fundamental theorem of algebra; Gödel 1931, already there; Hardy and Littlewood 1923, Goldbach's problem by the circle method; Hilbert 1899, the axioms of geometry proved independent; Hilbert 1901, the sphere of constant negative curvature that Euclidean space does not hold; Kripke 1975, the truth predicate; Löb 1955, the derivability conditions; Lobachevsky 1840, the other postulate adopted; Rosser 1936, ω-consistency removed; Turing 1936, computability; Turing 1939, the adding of Gödel sentences by ordinals. Each chapter carries one citation, of the edition the paper will cite, and the notes on what the publication is to the paper in the conversations' words with his quoted. Nine hold their text beside the chapter with a picture of its first page that opens it in another tab, their terms free: Beltrami as reprinted in his Opere, the pages cut from the Archive's scan; Dedekind in Beman's English; Feferman; Gauss in the first edition from e-rara; Gödel in Meltzer's; Hardy and Littlewood; Hilbert's Grundlagen in Townsend's and his paper of 1901 from JSTOR's early copy at the Archive; Lobachevsky in Halsted's. Five are cited by their address, under copyright: Kripke, Löb, Rosser and Turing's two; four of them open a copy a reader can read, Kripke's at IMPAN's truth seminar, Löb's at a Maryland course page, Turing 1936 at Virginia and Turing 1939 at Rutgers, on Doug's word that he is not selling this and wants versions he can read; Rosser's has none open beyond the publisher's and the Davis reprint. Chen is cited with no address, none found. Gödel's chapter came down to one file, Meltzer's, with the Collected Works named as the standard English, on Doug's word that we need the one we are going to cite and not versions.
The notes of the notebook that lean on a publication now say so and refer to its chapter, eleven references in five of them: The Documents to Löb, Turing 1939, Feferman and Rosser; The Mechanism to Lobachevsky, Beltrami, Hilbert 1901, the Grundlagen, Gauss, Dedekind and Turing 1936; Goldbach to Turing 1936; The Subject of Logic to Kripke; Two-Sided Arithmetic to Hardy and Littlewood and Chen. Two sentences were added to The Mechanism's geometric precedent for the works he added, Lobachevsky's postulate adopted outright and Hilbert's independence proofs, on his word that we need to talk about reasoning about what an axiom is. The library's table lists the fifteen under The Papers, its synopsis says what the book holds, and the prover walks every row, asking each held file and picture.
What the third sprint leaves
His review of the fifteen, which is why the list was cut to essentials. The five under copyright, read at their publishers; Chen's address. The rest of the references in The Papers stay references, each restored to a chapter on his word. The library's publishing, which waits on the repository's settings.
