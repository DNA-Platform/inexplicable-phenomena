The First Sprint
The first sprint of arithmetic, opened on 9 November 2026, when Doug named the subject: a paper he is working on, once called Goldbach versus Gödel, now arithmetic, with this project beside it to keep its sprints and plans, a textbook for its results and, in Mathematics, a library for the papers referenced.
What this sprint does. The structure first, every book a stub that can be walked: the subject, this project, the textbook and the library, built and proven the day they were named. Then a notebook, and the notes on Goldbach versus Gödel moved into it from Doug's own library, where the team has read and annotated four conversations with Claude: the planning of the paper, the semantics beside it, the symmetry of the coprimes, and the long conversation from the finite proof to the paper's flow. How the notebook is organized was decided as the notes arrived, and once more on Doug's correction: the notebook is the specification of the publication, fifteen chapters in four parts, the publication's own parts from the preface to Goldbach, the standing rulings and documents, the sketches set aside beside the paper, and the formalism, the options the notes hold and the algebraic model of the subject of logic on its way to PA, each note his words in the form that stands, citing what it deprecated and the conversation and chapter it came from, so the notes keep their track until he is happy with them and their organizing begins. And the team's book gains its chapter Doug, so that every book of this library is signed by him as the team's representative.
What it leaves for the next: the results, which go to the textbook once the notebook holds the notes they come from; the papers, which go to the library as they are referenced; and the form of this project's entries, which this one sets by being first.
