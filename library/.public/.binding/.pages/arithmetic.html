The Textbook of Arithmetic
The Textbook of Arithmetic
The specification of the Textbook of Arithmetic, 12 November 2026, amended 15 November.
1. The textbook
1.1 It holds the official results of arithmetic, stated and proved, and a result enters only as I have ruled it.
1.2 Its official form is the algebraic one.
1.3 Every chapter is titled by the thing it attempts to specify — a mathematical object as the starting point — and follows the entry (2).
2. The entry
2.1 It opens with the specification of its object, the sovereign part.
2.2 It carries an explanation.
2.3 It carries the formalism, annotated with justifications for how it satisfies the specification, citing items by number.
2.4 It carries presentations in contexts, each context specified.
2.5 It cites into the subject's library and the subject's notes.
2.6 It shows one aspect at a time.
2.7 Where something is specified and not yet built, a placeholder stands for it.
3. The chapters
3.1 A chapter is an attempt to specify one mathematical object, and bears its title.
3.2 The first object is named, 15 November: X, the symmetry of arithmetic, its chapter the textbook's first; further objects stand to be named, this item their placeholder, by 2.7.
3.3 The potential four chapters of 12 November are retired to the subject's notes.

The Notebook of Arithmetic
The notes of the paper, moved here from my library and organized as the specification of a publication: its parts from the preface to Goldbach, each in the form that stands and citing what it deprecated; the rulings and the documents; the sketches set aside with my tag on each; and the formalism, the options the notes hold, the algebraic model of the subject of logic I stated on 9 November, toward a compact expression and its formalization in PA, the connection found on 12 November between the subjective reading of Gödel and Riemann's exchange of zero and infinity, the definition of Gödel's system P looked up from the held paper toward deciding the paper's PA, the parts of the paper's argument, each with a sketch of its formalization and the references beside it, the bridesmaid analogy stored from my desktop session of 12 November, the unformalizable as always a composite and never the identity of the sentence, the first-order System A mapped into Gödel's P, drafted for the textbook and moved here on my ruling that it is a note, and the potential four chapters of 15 November, retired from the textbook in favor of specifications of mathematical objects as the starting point.
