The Book
What a book is here
Every book in this library extends the library's own, so what a book is here is decided once and a change to it reaches all of them. The book class is the layout: the masthead, the byline drawn from what the book's cover says, then the chapters. Its file is where the library's theme is registered, once, on the class every book extends. A cover's route opens at the top of the page; every other bookmark turns as the framework does.
The class stands beside this chapter as its code, borrowed on 2026-10-30 from the binder's own test library, whose manual the team wrote, and to be rebuilt from here by sketches rather than kept. The theme it registers is The Theme; the masthead and the byline it draws are The Masthead and the Byline.
The Theme
The values and the parts
A theme is a set of values, the font, the measure, the ink, the paper, the link, and the parts that use them: the page, the levels of a book, the labels, the links, the apparatus and the figures. The file beside this chapter is the one the book registers. It is the test library's theme as borrowed, a serif on warm paper, and it stands only until the sketches say what this library's own looks like.
The Masthead and the Byline
What a cover exposes
The running head names the library, the book and its table of contents, and the byline says who wrote the book, what it is about when the book is a subject's own, and its subject, all read from the cover and nowhere else. Neither is written in a chapter; the book draws them above its chapters from what the cover says. A subject is a book made one by its About, so the four books whose subject is Mathematics resolve to the book that is about it, and that book's byline says so. The file beside this chapter is both, borrowed from the test library and pointed at this library's name.
The Faces
A cover, a synopsis, a table
A face is how a book's apparatus looks: the cover a book opens on, the synopsis beside it, the table of contents that answers for its chapters and, when the book is a subject, for the books whose subject it is. Each is a word of the package dressed here, and every book of this library wears these four from the file beside this chapter, borrowed from the test library until a sketch replaces them.
The front
Since 10 November 2026 the cover is drawn as a book: the front, in the file beside this chapter, a board of a book's shape standing alone on the page while the cover is the open page, with the title, the author, the synopsis and the imprint on it, read from what the cover and the synopsis already say, as the byline is; its words are its links, the title opening the table of contents, the author and the subject their books, the library's name the library, and the running head, the byline and the cover's own title stand unseen behind it until another page opens. The theme draws it and gives a page that opens its one transition, a short fade. Sketch 004 of The Paper's Page, form a, by Doug's number.
Developing the Library
The loop
A save shows on an open page in under a second; a bind is the gate at the end of a piece of work and never the way to look during one. The workbench beside this chapter is the loop: it serves the library live from its sources, keeps one browser open on it, and answers a look with what it found and a photograph. Borrowed from the librarian's own library on 2026-10-30, its built address this library's.

The bind and the site
In the binding beside the site, npm run bind binds every book and leaves the site under the base the configuration names, and npm run preview with its port serves what the bind left. The site is committed as it is bound, and what is published is what was committed.

The Paper
A paper's page
A paper is a citable publication held in the library as a resource: the file itself beside a chapter of The Library of Mathematics, which carries the paper's citation and the notes its author writes on it, the paper signed by its own author. The page is minimal, on Doug's word of 10 November 2026: a small picture of the publication at the top left with the text flowing under it, the citation, the notes, and a link that opens the publication in another tab; the picture itself opens it. The page's form is sketch 002 of the design book, form a.
The plate and the opening
Two words in the file beside this chapter. A plate is the word that carries the picture, floated at the top left of the chapter by the theme; an opening is a reference that opens its address in another tab, a twin of the package's own reference with its anchor given a target. A chapter writes the plate first, before its title, as a word holding an opening whose text is the paper's literal and an image whose text is the picture's: the literals become the two files' addresses at the bind, so the picture is served beside the pages and the paper with it.
The picture
The picture is the paper's first page, made once by the tool beside this chapter: Chrome's own viewer draws the PDF with its toolbar and panes asked off, served to it by the tool since the viewer draws nothing opened from the disk as a file, photographed at a page's shape and cut to the page. The tool takes a page number for a scan of a bound book, which opens on its boards: Lobachevsky's title page is its fifth leaf. It stands beside the paper's chapter as a picture, named by the plate's literal, and is made again only when the paper changes.
