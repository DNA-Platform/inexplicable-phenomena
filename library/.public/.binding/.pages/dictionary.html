Definition
Semantic. A definition in Semantic Reference Theory is a statement that establishes the meaning of a term or concept by expressing its equivalence to a formal expression using the transduction operator.
Formal logic. A definition introduces a new symbol or term by asserting its logical equivalence to an expression containing only previously defined terms.
Linguistic. A definition establishes the semantic content of a word or phrase by stating its necessary and sufficient conditions.
Operational. A definition provides criteria for determining whether a concept applies to a given case, often through observable properties or procedures.
Related. Transduction, Semantic Reference Theory, Relationship, property, inheritance.

Identification
Linguistic. Identification is the process of recognizing or establishing the identity of something.
Semantic. Identification is the process by which a perspective assigns identity to a referent, enabling it to possess its own identity intrinsically. It is the first fundamental operation of a perspective.
Formal. (i) =(o)> (o) |=identify> (o) =(o)> o
Related. Perspective, qualification, relation, Identity, transduction.
Identity
Linguistic. Identity is the fact of being who or what a person or thing is; the characteristics determining this.
Semantic. Identity is the intrinsic quality that enables a referent to be itself and to be distinguishable from other referents. It is established through the process of Identification within a perspective.
Formal. (o) =(o)> o
Related. Identification, self-reference, property, perspective.

Object
Linguistic. An object is an entity toward which action, thought, or emotion is directed, often functioning as the grammatical object in a sentence.
Semantic. An object is a Referent that is the target of a Relationship. In the ternary relation R(x,y,t), y is the object.
Formal. E[x,y]: x => y =|> E[O]: y.O
Related. Subject, ternary, Relationship, Referent, property.
Reference
Linguistic. Reference is the relationship between a linguistic expression and the entity in the world that it identifies or denotes.
Semantic. Reference is the act by which a Subject directs attention to an Object through a specific Relationship type.
Formal. E[x,y,t]: x =t> y & x != y =|> x =ref> y
Related. Subject, Object, Relationship, symbol, Referent.
Referent
Semantic. A referent is an entity that can participate in the ternary relation R(x,y,t) as a subject, object, or relationship type. In the context of Semantic Reference Theory, a referent is defined as an object which could be assigned to a constant.
Formal. Referent(x) := "x can be substituted for a variable in SRT"
Philosophical. A referent is the target of reference, the object to which a name, sign, or description refers.
Ontological. A referent is a basic entity in a catalogue that maintains its identity through the relationships it participates in.
Related. Catalogue, Relationship, Subject, Object, constant, ternary, transduction.
Relationship
Semantic. A relationship is a connection between Referents established through the ternary relation R(x,y,t). When referents x and y are distinct, t specifies the relationship type that connects them.
Formal. R(x,y,t) & x != y, creating a directed connection from a subject to an object through a specific type.
Graph theory. A relationship can be viewed as a labeled, directed edge in a graph, where referents are nodes and the relationship type is the edge label.
Linguistic. A relationship corresponds to the predicate in a subject-predicate-object grammatical structure, determining how subjects relate to objects.
Related. Ternary, Subject, Object, property, inheritance, Referent, catalogue.

Subject
Linguistic. In grammar, a subject is the person or thing that performs the action expressed by a verb or about which something is stated.
Semantic. A subject is a Referent that participates in a direct Relationship with a distinct Object. In the ternary relation R(x,y,t), x is the subject when x and y differ.
Formal. E[x,y,t]: x =t> y =|> x != y =|> E[S]: x.S
Related. Ternary, Object, Relationship, Referent, perspective, property.
