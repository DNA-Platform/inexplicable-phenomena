Saggio di interpretazione della geometria non-euclidea
Eugenio Beltrami, Saggio di interpretazione della geometria non-euclidea, Giornale di Matematiche 6 (1868), 284–312; in English in John Stillwell, Sources of Hyperbolic Geometry, American Mathematical Society, 1996. Public domain; held beside this chapter as reprinted in his Opere matematiche, volume I, Milan: Hoepli, 1902, pages 374–405, the thirty-two pages cut from the Archive's scan of the volume. The Giornale's own pages are digitized at Göttingen and listed at the Deutsche Digitale Bibliothek, whose viewer answers a reader and refuses a script.
What the paper takes from it: the model, a surface of constant negative curvature on which the other geometry holds, so that the fifth postulate is not provable from the rest. "It has to be from geometry because that is where mathematicians reasoned their way to axioms like we are" (Goldbach through Gödel, The Geometric Precedent, 28 October 2026); in the precedents of The Mechanism, "Beltrami showed V unprovable".

On the Representation of a Large Even Integer
Chen Jing-run, On the representation of a larger even integer as the sum of a prime and the product of at most two primes, Scientia Sinica 16 (1973), 157–176; announced in Kexue Tongbao in 1966. Under copyright, and no address for its text was found on 10 November 2026, so it stands by its citation alone.
What the paper takes from it: the best unconditional output of the sieve, every large even number the sum of a prime and a number with at most two prime factors, and the barrier it marks. In the two sieves of Two-Sided Arithmetic: "the sieving limit of dimension two, Jurkat and Richert's lower bound vanishing exactly at full depth, the parity phenomenon under both, Chen's theorem the best unconditional output", which is why the paper's axiom is sited at the barrier rather than beyond it.

Continuity and Irrational Numbers
Richard Dedekind, Stetigkeit und irrationale Zahlen, Braunschweig: Vieweg, 1872; in English as Continuity and Irrational Numbers in Essays on the Theory of Numbers, translated by Wooster Woodruff Beman, Chicago: Open Court, 1901, public domain, held beside this chapter from Project Gutenberg, with the second essay, The Nature and Meaning of Numbers, in the same file.
What the paper takes from it: continuity adopted as an axiom in the author's own words, "I am utterly unable to adduce any proof of its correctness, nor has anyone" (Goldbach through Gödel, The Geometric Precedent, 28 October 2026), the precedent for how such an adoption is received; in the precedents of The Mechanism, "Dedekind on continuity and Cauchy on Poncelet's principle stand as the precedents for how such adoptions are received".

Arithmetization of Metamathematics in a General Setting
Solomon Feferman, Arithmetization of metamathematics in a general setting, Fundamenta Mathematicae 49 (1960), 35–92; open at the Polish digital mathematics library, from which the paper beside this chapter was taken.
What the paper takes from it: the intensionality of the consistency statement, that which sentence Con is depends on how provability is arithmetized, and the predicate under which a theory proves its own consistency. On Doug's word of 10 November 2026, Feferman and Turing are "the biggest names that my alternate interpretation of Godel chnages" (in the room, 10 November 2026). In the formal core of The Documents, formal equality is "the third criterion after neutrality and the fixed point, which Gödel's Con fails by Feferman's result and ε satisfies"; and in the frontier's result E, the progression of consistency statements lies in the frontier, "pairwise inequivalent and one point under the identity, so Turing and Feferman were enumerating names of ε".

Demonstratio nova theorematis
Carl Friedrich Gauss, Demonstratio nova theorematis omnem functionem algebraicam rationalem integram unius variabilis in factores reales primi vel secundi gradus resolvi posse, Helmstedt: Fleckeisen, 1799, the dissertation; reprinted in Werke, volume III, Göttingen, 1866, pages 1–30. Public domain; held beside this chapter in the first edition from e-rara's scan at ETH Zürich, which opens on e-rara's sheet with the dissertation behind it; the third volume of the Werke stands scanned at the Internet Archive.
What the paper takes from it: the first proof of the fundamental theorem of algebra, the field's version of the geometric precedent, "where the limit is an object and the sentence is also a theorem of analysis", as the precedents of The Mechanism keep it; on his word, "We want Hilbert's sphere and the fundamental theorem of algebra" (in the room, 10 November 2026).

On Formally Undecidable Propositions
Kurt Gödel, Über formal unentscheidbare Sätze der Principia Mathematica und verwandter Systeme I, Monatshefte für Mathematik und Physik 38 (1931), 173–198. In English as On Formally Undecidable Propositions of Principia Mathematica and Related Systems I, the standard translation being van Heijenoort's as revised in Kurt Gödel, Collected Works, volume I, Oxford, 1986, pages 144–195, with the German facing; read here in B. Meltzer's of 1962, with an introduction by R. B. Braithwaite, Oliver & Boyd and Basic Books, Dover 1992. The German original stands scanned at Essler's site.
What the paper takes from it: the whole of its first half. Gödel's theorems leave arithmetic a choice, add G, add ¬G, add consistency and its successors, or change nothing, and mathematics changed nothing and never said why; the paper says why, in The Preface. In the subject the paper builds, G and ¬G both have the consequence ε and neither is represented, so incompleteness is not meaningful there and no G is added, the reading of Zero for Proof; and the question the newest statement puts is which level the paper's Gödel half is written at, in The Options. Turing and Feferman are the names his reading of Gödel changes most, on Doug's word of 10 November 2026; the notes on the paper itself are his to extend here.

Some Problems of Partitio Numerorum, III
G. H. Hardy and J. E. Littlewood, Some problems of 'Partitio numerorum'; III: On the expression of a number as a sum of primes, Acta Mathematica 44 (1923), 1–70; open access at Acta Mathematica's archive and public domain in the United States, the paper beside this chapter taken from its mirror.
What the paper takes from it: Goldbach's problem as the circle method states it, and the singular series that the paper's count on the modular rings normalizes to; in the fourth movement of Two-Sided Arithmetic, "normalized, the count is the Hardy–Littlewood singular series" (Goldbach through Gödel, The Paper's Flow, 5 November 2026).

The Foundations of Geometry
David Hilbert, Grundlagen der Geometrie, Leipzig: Teubner, 1899; in English as The Foundations of Geometry, translated by E. J. Townsend, Chicago: Open Court, 1902, public domain, held beside this chapter from Project Gutenberg.
What the paper takes from it: the axioms of geometry proved independent of one another, each by a model in which it fails and the rest hold, which is what reasoning about an axiom looks like once the postulate is no longer Euclid's alone; the conversation's reading, that the Grundlagen "didn't just list axioms; it proved each one independent of the" others (Claude, The Shape of the Phantom Proof, 25 October 2026). In the precedents of The Mechanism it stands with Hilbert's sphere, the imaginary sphere shown no object in Euclidean space.

On Surfaces of Constant Gaussian Curvature
David Hilbert, Ueber Flächen von constanter Gaussscher Krümmung, Transactions of the American Mathematical Society 2 (1901), 87–99, at the Society; public domain, held beside this chapter from JSTOR's early journal copy at the Archive, which opens on JSTOR's sheet with the paper behind it; reprinted as Appendix V of the Grundlagen der Geometrie from its second edition.
What the paper takes from it: Hilbert's sphere, that no complete surface of constant negative curvature stands in Euclidean space, so the sphere of imaginary radius Lambert read the acute case off is no object there; in the precedents of The Mechanism, "Hilbert showed the imaginary sphere no object in Euclidean space"; on his word, "We want Hilbert's sphere and the fundamental theorem of algebra" (in the room, 10 November 2026).

Outline of a Theory of Truth
Saul Kripke, Outline of a Theory of Truth, The Journal of Philosophy 72 (1975), 690–716, at JSTOR, under copyright and read there.
What the paper takes from it: the price of a truth predicate with gaps, some sentences getting no value, named on the first day as what dropping the excluded middle would cost, and declined. The subject of logic keeps the law and lets it evaluate to the identity: "G and not G have the empty proof, so G and not G are Con(PA) and Con(PA) which is just Con(PA) which we already know anyways" (Goldbach through Gödel, The Identity Element, 28 October 2026), as The Subject of Logic gathers it, with Kripke's price named beside it (Claude, Certainty, 19 October 2026).

Solution of a Problem of Leon Henkin
M. H. Löb, Solution of a problem of Leon Henkin, The Journal of Symbolic Logic 20 (1955), 115–118, at Cambridge, under copyright and read there.
What the paper takes from it: the derivability conditions a provability predicate must satisfy, which the lifted predicate in PA and the identity's rule of The Options are measured against, and Löb's theorem itself, that a theory proving its own reflection for a sentence proves the sentence. In the frontier's results of The Documents, result D: "for unprovable S, the reflection sentence Prov(S) → S is in the frontier, Löb's theorem as a closure property".

Geometrical Researches on the Theory of Parallels
Nikolai Lobachevsky, Geometrische Untersuchungen zur Theorie der Parallellinien, Berlin: Fincke, 1840; in English as Geometrical Researches on the Theory of Parallels, translated by George Bruce Halsted, Austin: University of Texas, 1891, public domain, held beside this chapter from a scan of the Austin printing at Lorraine.
What the paper takes from it: the other postulate adopted outright and a geometry built on it, the precedent for reasoning about what an axiom is. On his word: "I would add work on geometry where different postulates were adopted to justify a different geometry because we need to talk about reasoning about what is an axiom" (in the room, 10 November 2026); in the precedents of The Mechanism, it stands beside Lambert's acute case and Beltrami's model.
Read the book ↗, Halsted's English.
Extensions of Some Theorems of Gödel and Church
J. Barkley Rosser, Extensions of some theorems of Gödel and Church, The Journal of Symbolic Logic 1 (1936), 87–91, at Cambridge, under copyright and read there; reprinted in Martin Davis, The Undecidable, Raven 1965, Dover 2004. No open copy of the pages was found on 10 November 2026.
What the paper takes from it: the first theorem freed of ω-consistency by Rosser's sentence, which the paper reads as hiding the belief rather than removing it, since one cannot quantify over an infinite domain without a belief about representation (Goldbach through Gödel, Certainty, 19 October 2026). In the frontier's results of The Documents, result H: a sentence is in the frontier exactly when the theory with it and the theory with its negation are both consistent, "so undecidable means two subjects of one empty representation, and by Rosser every consistent subject has a name for its identity".
On Computable Numbers
A. M. Turing, On Computable Numbers, with an Application to the Entscheidungsproblem, Proceedings of the London Mathematical Society, second series, 42 (1937), 230–265, read in 1936, at the Society; public domain in Britain since 2025 and under copyright in the United States until 2032, read there.
What the paper takes from it: computability, and the form of a thesis. The circular reasoning thesis is stated in Church and Turing's form: "Any universals that can be proven should literally be machine checkable. The Doug's Circular Reasoning Thesis should be like the Church Turing Thesis" (Goldbach through Gödel, The Circular Reasoning Thesis, 27 October 2026), as The Mechanism says; and the paper's reading of Goldbach's conjecture as an axiom is, as a deduction, "invalid and is meant to be: a thesis in the sense of Church and Turing, confirmed by every independent formalization through chart invariance and by every instance", as Goldbach says. On Doug's word of 10 November 2026, Turing and Feferman are "the biggest names that my alternate interpretation of Godel chnages" (in the room, 10 November 2026).

Systems of Logic Based on Ordinals
A. M. Turing, Systems of Logic Based on Ordinals, Proceedings of the London Mathematical Society, second series, 45 (1939), 161–228, at the Society; public domain in Britain since 2025 and under copyright in the United States until 2035, read there.
What the paper takes from it: the adding of Gödel sentences, the progression of theories each taking the consistency of the last as an axiom, along the ordinals, which Feferman carried on. The paper reads the whole progression as one point: in the frontier's results of The Documents, result E, "the progression Con, Con(PA + Con), and on, lies in the frontier, pairwise inequivalent and one point under the identity, so Turing and Feferman were enumerating names of ε". On Doug's word of 10 November 2026, Turing and Feferman are "the biggest names that my alternate interpretation of Godel chnages" (in the room, 10 November 2026).
