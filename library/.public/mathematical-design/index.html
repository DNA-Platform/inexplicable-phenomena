The Paper's Page
Sketch 001, four forms of a paper's page
The first sketch of The Library of Mathematics asks what a paper's page should look like, a paper being a citable publication held in the library as a resource beside a chapter that Doug writes, the chapter carrying the citation and the paper read on the page. The page is snapshotted off the bound library's stub, its sheets inlined and its scripts stripped, the paper's row put in the table and the paper's chapter after it, and appended to this chapter with its forms on a switch; a form is a question, not a design. The forms: a, the browser's own viewer in place, below the citation and the description, at the chapter's measure and four fifths of the window tall; b, the viewer above the fold at a page's height with the description beside it, and the title's citation opening the paper in a new tab; c, the first page as text, Hirzel's words through pdftotext in the code figure, the viewer behind a press; and t, the table, the paper's row as key, title and year under a heading of its own.
What the sketch found on its first runs: the library opens one page at a time and hides the rest, the cover by default, so the first photographs showed the cover and nothing of the chapter or the row until the switch learned to open the page each form is about, the paper's for a, b and c and the table's for t, as a reader arriving at the address would see it. In headless Chrome the viewer's frame draws, its toolbar and its loading line, but the paper does not load from a page opened as a file, so every photograph shows the frame dark where the pages will be; opened in a browser from the file, the viewer loads the paper from its address on the web, which is how the sketch is to be looked at. Form c's text falls into the code figure's monospace and reads as a listing and not as a page, since the library has no figure for a page of prose taken from a file; if the text is wanted on the page, that figure is owed. The row's year doubles the key, Gödel 1931 and 1931 again. Nothing here moved into the manual.

Sketch 002, the minimal page
The second sketch follows his answer to the first, the same day: not a frame or a look to commit to, a library built as utility minimally and grown as needed; the table listing the publications in alphabetical order and reached easily, a nexus of catalogues eventually; a chapter a minimal place to write notes about the publication with a link that opens it, possibly in another tab; and the preview he liked kept as a small picture of the publication at the top left of the page, clicked to open it. The viewer at the measure comes out. The picture is the paper's first page, made once by Chrome's own viewer with its toolbar and panes asked off and cropped to the page, a tool of the sprint's scratch until a form is chosen. The forms: a, the picture floated at the top left with the title and the citation beside it and the notes flowing under it once past; b, the picture in a column of its own with nothing flowing under; t, the cover and the table on one page, the papers in alphabetical order, two rows the references entry names marked to come so the order shows.

Sketch 003, the cover
His number on 002 was a, the picture at the top left with the text flowing under it; and on its table he asked instead for the cover: a transition when the book is first opened, the feel of a book, not unlike the paper's picture but bigger, in ideas that match the current theme's feel. The third sketch puts three ideas of the cover on a switch, each a board built from the page's own words in the theme's Georgia on white with its small spaced capitals and hairline rules, and each opening to the contents with a transition when clicked, the board giving way upward and the table coming in beneath; the switch's last press closes the book again. The forms: a, the front board, a panel tall as a book framed by a double rule with a spine's shadow at its left, the title large on it, the synopsis in italic, the author and the subject at its foot; b, the shelf, the same board carrying the publications as their pictures along a shelf at its foot, each a way into its chapter; c, the half-title, the title alone on a blank leaf with no frame, which turns rather than lifts when opened.

What was built
Of the four, two moved into the manual by his number on 10 November 2026: the paper's page is 002's form a, the plate and the opening of The Paper, and the cover is 004's form a, the front of The Faces; 001 and 003 stand here as the questions they were. Two departures from the decided HTML, kept on purpose: the title stands whole on the front, in two lines, where the sketch set THE LIBRARY OF small above the name, since a title is one writing of the library's and not two; and the picture is cut to the paper's own page shape, read from the PDF, where the sketch's was cut to A4's.
Sketch 004, the book's cover
His word on 003, "It's good", and three corrections: the author and the subject on the cover itself, no header above it, the shape of a book cover, and a click anywhere on the book opening the contents. So the fourth sketch shows the cover alone on its page, the running head and the byline gone while the book is closed and back when it opens; the book two by three, narrower than the measure, standing in the middle of the page with a spine's shadow and a double rule, the title large on it, the author under the title as a reference to Our Team, the synopsis in italic, and at the foot the subject as a reference to Our Math and the library's name; a click anywhere on the book, save on a link, lifting it away as the contents come in beneath. Two forms kept, a the front board and b the shelf, which carries the publications as their pictures along a shelf above the foot; the half-title dropped, since it carried no author.
