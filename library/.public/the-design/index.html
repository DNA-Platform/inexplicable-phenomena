The Top Page
Sketch 001, three forms of the top page
The first sketch asks what the top of the library should look like, from the bound print of the top book as the borrowed manual draws it. The page is appended to this chapter with its forms on a switch, and a form is a question, not a design. The forms: a, the page as bound, the cover in its box, the byline above it, the synopsis beside, the catalogue as a two-column table; b, a front, the masthead and the byline on one line, the name large, the synopsis as a standfirst, the catalogue as three shelves side by side and the chapter below; c, the subjects first, the catalogue standing above the synopsis in two columns under a small cover.
What the sketch found on its first runs: the header row of the catalogue, Book and What it is, is a row like the others and became a shelf of its own in form b until the sheet hid it by a rule more specific than the one that made the shelves, since a later rule of equal weight had won. And form c misfired: the masthead, the byline and the chapters are not siblings on the page, each standing in a container of its own, so an order given to them put the masthead last and left the catalogue under the synopsis; a form that moves the parts needs another mechanism than order. Nothing here moved into the manual.

The Manual's Page
The listing, five forms of the manual's page
The first sketch of the manual's page asks how a chapter of Our Reference shows the file beside it, the code that is the part the chapter specifies, now that the manual is written as a specification. The listing itself is to look as it does in my own library's manual, measured off it on 17 November 2026: JetBrains Mono at twelve pixels on a line of twenty, the ink pale on a night of deep teal, the keywords sky, the strings amber, the names mint, the comments dimmed, the numbers in a column of brass, and that manual's light palette behind a switch. The container is this page's own, the editor's bar speaking in the page's label voice. The page is snapshotted off 4343's print of The Faces, its sheets inlined and its scripts stripped, and appended to this chapter with its forms on a switch, each form named for what it is since I asked for semantic names the same day; a form is a question, not a design. The forms: the plate, the editor standing in the chapter where the code figure stands today, at the measure, capped at seven tenths of the window and scrolling within, with light, wrap and lines on its bar and a full screen that takes the window; the back, the chapter's words alone on its front and the file on its back, flipped by the square at the title's right that The Content flips with, the title turning into the file's own name in the editor's face as the content's title turns into its literal; the spread, my library's container on this paper, the words at the window's left and the editor beside them to its edge, the height of the window; and on the table's page, the foot, a section at the bottom of the contents in the smaller voice, one row a file under the chapter it stands beside, and under the chapters, each file a press under its chapter's row with the file's mark.
What the sketch found on its first runs: the plate stood below the fold in every photograph until the switch learned to scroll to it, as a reader arriving at the code would; the spread gridded the pagination's container instead of the theme's and left the words centred, until the sheet was named by its place under the root; the figure on the print carries its highlighting already, 2,315 spans, and no line numbers, since the manual never asked for them, so the sketch numbers the lines as the code figure would; a chapter with two files beside it, The Paper's code and its preview tool, shows one tab, since only the code is a figure on the page; and on the phone the bar's four words wrap to a second line. My word on it, the same day: the sketch is too linear; try full screen without breaking the feel; keep the paper yet have something more like mine without going into the web app. Nothing here moved into the manual.
The photographs of the listing
The opening, the two modes of the manual
The second sketch follows my answer to the first, the same day: a full screen versus a page mode for the reference manual, as the code has, so it can be book-like and editor-like, and as an editor it still needs to feel bookish and paperly; and the appendix is a special part of a book, written with the reference manual's components, that refers to the code specific to the book. The same print, with the modes on the editor's bar where the listing's full screen stood, the one in force pressed. The forms: the page, the book-like mode, the chapter as bound with the plate at its foot; the opening, the editor-like mode, the book open flat across the window, the chapter's words on the left page with the running head and the chapter's line as they are, and on the right a page of its own, a running head naming the file beside the chapter and the editor under it the height of the page; the leaf, the file as a page of the book alone, the running head over it and the chapter's line turned into the file's name, the editor across the window beneath; and on the table's page, the foot, the appendix as a part of the book at the bottom of the contents in the smaller voice, its chapters as rows and each file a press beneath its chapter. The opening and the leaf take the light palette behind the bar's switch, paper on both pages then.
What the sketch found on its first runs: the leaf's running head, cloned outside the theme's own container, lost every rule the theme gives it and showed both of its titles at once, until the leaf's page was stood inside the sheet where the page's rules reach; the left page keeps the measure centred in its half of the window, so the words sit where a page's words sit; and the first drawing gave the two pages a gutter's shadow and the editor a drop shadow, which my word struck the same day: look at the other pages, not bookish with shading, paperish as in unfinished, the white paper and the editor with its options being the breathtaking contrast. My other answers: the appendix can be another part of the table, since it is kind of different; the editor view uses the table as the file tree; and the word recto goes as unclear. Nothing here moved into the manual.

The editor, the table as the tree
The third sketch follows my answers to the second, the same day, and the aesthetic of the library's other pages, captured off 4343: white, Georgia, one hairline under the running head, the teal on the head and the links alone, and the cover's board the one thing on any page with a shadow. So every shadow is struck from the three sketches, and the editor view is three columns of one sheet: the contents down the left in the page's own voice, every chapter a row and every file beneath its chapter with the file's mark, the open chapter marked by a hairline of the teal; the chapter's words beside them at a narrower measure; and the file's page at the right under a running head naming it, the editor the height of the window beneath. The modes on the editor's bar: words, which puts the words away so the tree and the file stand alone, as my library's words tab does; the page; and the editor. The forms: the editor, the editor with the words away, the page as bound with the plate, and the foot, the appendix as a part at the bottom of the table; the light palette behind the bar's switch as before.
What the sketch found on its first runs: the editor's bar cannot shrink below its words, so the bar and the pane under it stretched past the editor's edge and the page past the window, measured at 578 in an editor 525 wide, until the editor's one column was told to shrink and the bar to wrap; at the desk's width the bar then wraps to two rows in the three-column view and stays on one in the opening; the file's page takes a little more of the window than the words, the tree thirteen rems and the words at most thirty-six, so a line of code has 570 pixels before it scrolls. Nothing here moved into the manual.

The Project's Page
The sketch of the parts
The first sketch of the project's page asks how the five parts of a project — specification, identification, justification, intuition, progress — stand on one page in their own forms, with views chosen instead of scrolled. It is built from Sprint 130's own record, the sprint rendered as its own exploration. The views bar shows all of the parts or one; two squares in the toggle's dress switch the layers — annotations, the page's object model drawn: every justification, the connecting principle and the Archive strike are data, a relation drawing at both of its ends with its one writing, the coverage computed from what no relation claims; and references, the citations that join the parts and drain to ink when hidden. Each section's heading carries a small square that toggles its own annotations, and the bar's square is every section's at once. The specification renders its clauses in the literal markdown of The Specification, coverage in the margin reading satisfied or open; the identification is five anchored statements, each the form a part takes, a justified statement ruled at its left; the intuition is a small graph of principles, stated so they can be compared, where a relationship between two principles is itself a principle; the progress is a journal of bylined, dated entries, one really struck by the Archive mark that closed it. The forms: registers, each part in the voice its meaning first suggested — legal, formal, essay, journal; and integrated, on my word mid-sketch that everything in the project part should at least share a font, though not necessarily in markdown form — the parts in one source face, the markdown's dashes gone, its marks softened, the whole smoother and subtler. And the structure underneath is interactive: a press on a justification's mark turns the page into that justification in its own right — its clauses, possibly several, and its entry travelling to stand face to face with the argument between them — and a press on the connecting principle stands the edge between its ends; the page is one projection of the project's graph, and a press moves to another.
The twelfth round specified the specification's own tree, and the sketch took it the same hour: a specification always has numbers or codes, always has nesting, and the numbers nest — 4.2 standing under 4 — not only a list of lists, though it can be imagined as one. The operations came with it: a section folds on its heading's press and stands with a quiet count of what folded; any part hides entirely from a small mark in its gutter, Show all standing in the section's heading until everything is back; a part drags to stand beside another for comparing, its code and its marks travelling with it because the code carries identity; and the annotations toggle on what is visible — a folded or hidden clause draws nothing, its marks returning with it, while an opened connection overrides the tree, a hidden clause standing again while its justification is open. The same hour the tree went into the manual: the thirteenth round ruled that The Specification should have this code, so it gained its ninth section and an appendix holding the tree as an object and the page as its rendering, run on that specification itself, while The Content took the five parts and the annotation foundation as its own sections.
What the sketch found: a dotted underline across a justified statement read loud and became the pale rule at its left; with references off the teal drains to ink and every part still reads whole, which is the test that references are connection and not content; a citation pressed in one view turns the page to all of them and lands on its target, the two-way navigation a coverage view will need; the margin marks have no phone answer yet, since at the phone's width there is no margin to stand in; and the struck entry keeps its strike when the annotation layer is off, the strike being the entry's state and the note only its explanation. When the marks moved into the object model, the computed coverage corrected the hand: clause 4.2, written open, is claimed by J3 and the model says so. A ruling fell on hierarchy as the sketch was photographed: the title of a section needs to be more important, and markdown's own headings should hardly exceed the body — a little bold, not much, partially rendered — said for this page and for the arithmetic specification alike, so the correction went into The Content in the same stroke. The move-to-compare answered a question by construction: 4.2 dragged beside 7.3 — the two clauses one justification claims — seats a relation's two objects together with the same J3 mark on each, which is what comparing is for; though the margin crowds where two long marks meet, the collision joining the phone among the margin's opens.
